Accessibility Statement

Designed to be easier to read, tap, hear, and navigate.

Senior Care Compass serves older adults, caregivers, families, and people who may have visual, motor, cognitive, or hearing disabilities. Accessibility is part of the product design, not an afterthought.

Last updated: September 27, 2026

Current accessibility features

  • Large-text control
  • High-contrast control
  • Browser-based read-aloud control where supported
  • Large tap targets and mobile-first layouts
  • Keyboard-focus styles and semantic buttons/links
  • Skip-to-content link
  • Labels for ZIP input and questionnaire controls
  • Text explanations that do not rely only on color
  • Responsive layouts designed for phone screens

Our accessibility goal

We aim to improve the site using recognized accessible-design practices, including principles found in WCAG. This statement is not a certification that every page or third-party destination fully conforms to a particular accessibility standard.

Known limitations

Read-aloud behavior depends on the visitor’s browser/device speech engine. Third-party sites and government/nonprofit destinations linked from Senior Care Compass control their own accessibility. Public provider data can also include long organization names or addresses that are harder to read on small screens.

Ongoing improvement

As features are added, we will continue checking keyboard operation, text scaling, contrast, headings, form labels, focus visibility, readable error messages, and mobile usability. A dedicated accessibility contact method should be published when the final site operator/contact channel is established.

Accessibility reference: U.S. Department of Justice web-accessibility guidance describes accessible web content as important for equal access and identifies practices such as sufficient color contrast and text cues.
