Compassionate after-loss guidance

When a Loved One Passes

We know this can feel overwhelming. Take one step at a time. This guide organizes the first decisions, consumer rights, cost questions, benefits, and places to ask for help.

Start with the first 24–48 hours

From our family to yours: created with care by Edwin & Al Roman and the Roman family, inspired by our mother, born 1937, who is still with us and remains at the heart of Senior Care Compass.

Reviewed September 27, 2026. Federal rights and benefits below were checked against FTC, Social Security, VA, and 988 sources. State and local rules can be different, so confirm local requirements.
1. Immediate checklist

First 24–48 hours

Not every family will need every step. Expected hospice deaths, hospital deaths, and unexpected deaths are handled differently.

📞

Call the right help first

If the death is unexpected, suspicious, violent, or there is any doubt the person may still be alive, call 911. If death was expected under hospice care, call the hospice nurse first and follow the care plan. Local rules determine pronouncement and reporting.

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦

Call family, clergy, or a support person

Ask someone you trust to sit with you, take notes, make calls, and help you compare options. You do not have to handle every decision alone.

⏸️

Do not rush belongings

Unless safety, housing, landlord, estate, or legal deadlines require action, you usually do not need to sort belongings immediately. Secure valuables and give yourself time.

📄

Gather important papers

Look for the will, advance directives, life insurance, Social Security number, birth and marriage records, DD214 if a Veteran, bank information, and any prepaid funeral or cemetery contract.

⚖️

Compare funeral providers

Under the FTC Funeral Rule, you can ask for accurate price information by phone and receive a written General Price List when you visit. Comparing several providers is allowed and can reduce pressure.

🏛️

Social Security & benefits

Funeral homes generally report deaths to Social Security. Surviving family members should still contact SSA promptly to ask about the $255 lump-sum death payment and possible monthly survivor benefits.

🏠

Secure home, pets, mail & medications

Arrange pet care, secure the home and valuables, safeguard medications, and deal with mail or utilities only as needed. Keep a simple list of calls and documents.

💛

Grief and crisis support

Grief itself is not a crisis. If grief becomes a mental-health or suicide crisis, call or text 988. Veterans and service members can call 988 and press 1. For ongoing grief support, ask local hospice programs, faith communities, 211, or community counseling services.

🛡️ Your FTC Funeral Rule rights

  • Phone prices: funeral providers must give accurate price information over the phone when you ask.
  • General Price List: when you inquire in person, you must be offered a written itemized GPL that you can keep.
  • Choose only what you want: you may buy individual goods and services, subject to the basic services fee and any applicable legal, cemetery, or crematory requirements.
  • Third-party caskets: a funeral home cannot refuse a casket bought elsewhere or charge a special handling fee just because it came from another seller.
  • Embalming: it is not always required by law. Ask why it is being recommended or required for the arrangements you chose.
  • Online prices: as of this review, the federal Funeral Rule does not universally require funeral homes to post their GPL online. Many do so voluntarily; state rules may be stricter.

3. Choosing an arrangement

Burial, cremation, green options & Veterans benefits

There is no single “right” choice. Family wishes, faith, cost, environmental preferences, timing, and local availability all matter.

⚰️

Traditional burial

May include viewing, ceremony, casket, cemetery burial, and memorialization. Ask separately about cemetery plot, vault, opening/closing, and marker costs.

🔥

Direct cremation

A simpler disposition without a funeral-home viewing or ceremony. Families can hold a memorial later at a home, church, park, or other location.

🕯️

Cremation with memorial

Combines cremation with a memorial service before or after. Timing is flexible and the urn may or may not be present.

🌿

Green / natural burial

May avoid embalming and use biodegradable materials. Availability, cemetery rules, and total costs vary widely by location.

💧

Alkaline hydrolysis / water cremation

Availability and legal status vary by state. Confirm directly with your state regulator and a licensed provider before relying on this option.

🇺🇸

Veterans burial & memorial benefits

Eligible Veterans may receive national cemetery burial benefits including a gravesite, opening/closing, perpetual care, government headstone/marker, burial flag, and Presidential Memorial Certificate at no cost. Funeral-home services remain the family’s responsibility. Separate burial allowances may also be available.

5. Financial help & grief support

Benefits and people who can help

💰 Social Security

A qualifying spouse or some children may receive the one-time $255 death payment, and eligible family members may qualify for monthly survivor benefits. Apply promptly.

SSA: What to do when someone dies ↗

🇺🇸 Veterans

Burial allowances and national cemetery benefits depend on eligibility, service connection, and date of death. Current VA rates change, so verify them directly rather than relying on an old fixed dollar amount.

VA burial allowance ↗

☎️ Local assistance

Call 211 to ask about county or nonprofit help with funeral costs, food, housing, transportation, or other urgent family needs. Availability varies by community.

💛 Grief support

Ask local hospice programs, counseling centers, faith communities, hospitals, senior centers, or 211 about bereavement groups. If grief becomes a crisis or you feel unsafe, call or text 988.
