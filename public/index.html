Immediate care

Emergency — what to do right now

If there is trouble breathing, chest pain, possible stroke, a serious fall, sudden severe confusion, or another immediate danger, call 911. Do not drive yourself in an emergency.

FAST for stroke

Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call 911.

Fall at home

Do not lift someone alone if there is severe pain or possible injury. Keep them safe and tell responders about blood thinners or important medicines.

After the ER

Keep discharge papers, medication changes, follow-up instructions, and the name of the clinician or clinic to contact.

Practical resource hub

Help for the parts of senior care families deal with every day

Open any section. After a ZIP search, location-sensitive headings will update to the ZIP you entered.

How to find care fast

  • Use the live ZIP finder above for Medicare hospitals, nursing homes, clinics, and pharmacies.
  • Medicare Care Compare — compare Medicare-certified providers.
  • HRSA Find a Health Center — locate community health centers.
  • Ask your primary-care office whether home visits, telehealth, or home health may be appropriate.

What to bring to any visit

  • Photo ID and insurance cards
  • Current medication list or pill bottles
  • Allergies, recent falls, and emergency contact
  • A short list of questions you need answered

Questions to ask the clinician

  • What are the most important warning signs?
  • Could any medicines cause dizziness or interactions?
  • Who should we call after hours?
  • What follow-up should happen and when?

Mobile & home-based care

Availability varies by area and insurance. Ask Medicare Care Compare, your health plan, primary-care office, or Area Agency on Aging for local options.

Find pharmacies

Use the live ZIP finder above for NPI-registered pharmacy organizations. Call to confirm hours, delivery, insurance, and whether the location is open to the public.

Medication assistance

NeedyMeds lists patient-assistance resources. Medicare Extra Help may reduce Part D costs for eligible people.

Medication safety

  • Keep one current medication list
  • Include OTC medicines and supplements
  • Use one pharmacy when practical
  • Ask before stopping or changing a prescription

Meals on Wheels

Use Meals on Wheels America to find a local program. Services and eligibility vary locally.

Food assistance

Feeding America can help locate food banks. Call 211 for local pantries and delivery programs.

Delivery tip

When using grocery delivery, save common items, add substitutions, and make sure the senior has a clear plan for receiving refrigerated or frozen food.

Local help

Call 211 for community resources and Eldercare Locator at 1-800-677-1116 to connect with the Area Agency on Aging serving the ZIP you entered.

Safety & protection

  • Emergency or immediate danger: 911
  • Suspected elder abuse: ask Eldercare Locator for the correct Adult Protective Services office
  • Scams and fraud: FTC ReportFraud
  • For police non-emergency matters, use the official local department number

Benefits screening

BenefitsCheckUp can screen for multiple programs. Eligibility rules vary by program and location.

Food & utilities

Explore SNAP and LIHEAP. State and local agencies make eligibility determinations.

Medicare & prescriptions

Extra Help can reduce Medicare Part D costs for eligible people. Medicare Savings Programs may also help with Medicare costs.

Phone service

Lifeline is the federal communications assistance program. Check current eligibility and support directly with the official program.

Veteran benefits

VA Aid & Attendance / Housebound may increase pension benefits for eligible veterans or survivors who meet VA requirements.

Medicaid & local relief

Medicaid home- and community-based services, property-tax relief, and other programs are state-specific. Start with your state Medicaid agency, 211, or Eldercare Locator.

Start with official information

  • Medicare Care Compare for Medicare-certified nursing homes, home health, hospice, and other providers
  • Your state licensing agency for assisted-living inspection/licensing information
  • Eldercare Locator for local aging-service guidance

Questions to ask on a tour

  1. What is included in the monthly price?
  2. What costs extra as needs increase?
  3. How is overnight staffing handled?
  4. How are falls, emergencies, and hospital transfers handled?
  5. What are the move-out or discharge criteria?
  6. How are medication and memory-care needs handled?

Staying home longer

  • Home-safety assessment
  • Meal and pharmacy delivery
  • Transportation plan
  • Home-care or home-health evaluation when appropriate
  • Backup plan for caregiver absence

Compare total cost

Do not compare only the base monthly rate. Include added care fees, medication support, transportation, supplies, private-duty care, and likely future increases.

Food banks

Feeding America can locate food banks serving your area.

Senior nutrition

Ask the local Area Agency on Aging about congregate meals, home-delivered meals, and nutrition programs for older adults.

USDA programs

Programs such as SNAP, CSFP, and the Senior Farmers' Market Nutrition Program have specific eligibility and state/local availability rules. Use official USDA or state sources.

Daily care log

  • Date and sleep
  • Meals and fluids
  • Medications taken
  • Pain, mood, bathroom changes
  • Falls or new symptoms

Medication tracker

  • Medication name
  • Dose and time
  • Reason for taking it
  • Prescriber and pharmacy
  • Allergies or reactions

5-minute fall check

  • Secure or remove loose rugs
  • Nightlights in halls and bathroom
  • Proper grab bars near toilet and shower
  • Clear walking paths
  • Ask about dizziness or balance problems

Emergency sheet

  • Doctors and phone numbers
  • Medication and allergy list
  • Insurance information
  • Advance directive location
  • Family contact and preferred hospital

Caregiver backup plan

  • Who can step in for one day?
  • Who has keys and medication information?
  • What happens if the caregiver becomes ill?
  • Which respite options are available?

Use at appointments

  • Bring a short concern list
  • Write down medication changes
  • Ask what symptoms require urgent care
  • Confirm the next appointment or test

Care planning

Four questions to help organize the conversation

This does not diagnose a condition or decide placement. It gives your family a practical care category to discuss with qualified professionals.

Senior care questionnaire

Question 1 of 4About 2 minutes

1. What is the current living situation?

Choose the option that best reflects the senior's situation today.

2. What is the primary concern?

Pick the issue creating the greatest need for support.

3. How would you describe the current health status?

Think about day-to-day health and how much help is normally required.

4. What level of support is desired?

Select the level closest to what the family is looking for.
